## 📑 Key Concepts
*   **Domain:** All possible x-values (inputs).
*   **Range:** All possible y-values (outputs).
*   **Indeterminate forms to avoid in Domain:**
    *   Division by zero: `Denominator ≠ 0`
    *   Square root of negative: `Expression inside √ ≥ 0`

## **Topic 1: Functions, Domain, and Range**

### **Theoretical Framework and Rules**

A function ![](../assets/image1.png) is a mathematical relation that maps every element in a domain space ![](../assets/image2.png) to one unique element in a range space ![](../assets/image3.png).1 The topological constraints of these mappings are strictly dictated by the arithmetic operations within the function itself. The domain comprises all real input values that do not induce undefined mathematical conditions, such as division by zero or the even root of a negative scalar.2 The range represents the subsequent projection of all possible output values.2 Understanding domain boundaries is paramount when transitioning to continuous calculus; differentiability strictly requires continuity, and continuity cannot exist outside the domain space.3 In applied physical sciences, domain boundaries typically represent absolute physical constraints, such as the event horizon of a black hole, absolute zero temperature limits, or the speed of light barrier.
