## 🏛️ Week 1: Society, Science, and Technology

### Definition and Uses
* **Society:** An organized group of individuals within a defined territory sharing common cultural patterns, values, and institutions. It provides the social environment for nurturing human potential.
* **Science:** A systematic body of knowledge obtained through observation and experimentation to explain natural and social phenomena. It primarily answers the question *"why"*.
* **Technology:** The practical application of scientific knowledge to design tools and systems that solve problems. It focuses on *"how"* to satisfy human needs.
* **Distinction:** Science is theoretical and concerned with discovering truth; technology is practical and concerned with creating useful products.

### Technological Determinism
* **Driving Force:** Technology acts as a powerful agent of social change, reshaping social structures, economic activities, and cultural practices.
* **Circular Relationship:** Society generates needs that stimulate scientific research, which is then transformed into technology. These technological changes, in turn, alter social life and create new challenges.
* **Social Transformation:** In modern eras, science and technology influence how people learn, work, govern, and perceive reality.

## 📊 Week 2: Social Research Methods

### Basic Concepts & Methods
* **Quantitative Research:** Uses numbers and statistics, often through surveys or questionnaires, to collect data from large samples.
* **Qualitative Research:** Focuses on words and meanings through methods like in-depth interviews or participant observation (ethnography) to provide rich, detailed descriptions.
* **Mixed Methods:** Many studies combine both approaches for a more comprehensive understanding of social issues.

### Stages of Social Research (6–8 Step Lifecycle)
1. **Topic Selection:** Choosing a relevant issue or defining a problem.
2. **Review of Literature:** Reading existing studies to build on current knowledge.
3. **Formulating Questions/Hypotheses:** Turning topics into clear, testable questions.
4. **Research Design:** Planning methods, sample size, and ethical considerations.
5. **Sampling:** Selecting a representative subset of the population.
6. **Data Collection:** Gathering data systematically through fieldwork.
7. **Data Analysis:** Organizing data (e.g., using SPSS for quantitative) and finding patterns.
8. **Reporting:** Drawing conclusions and sharing findings for policy or academic use.

## 🎭 Week 3: Culture and Civilization

### Culture vs. Civilization
* **Culture:** The "software" of society, including shared patterns of behavior, beliefs, and values. It consists of *material culture* (tangible objects like tools and technology) and *non-material culture* (intangible ideas, laws, and morals).
* **Civilization:** A more advanced stage of cultural development marked by urbanization, writing systems, and centralized governance. It is the "grand edifice" built on the foundation of culture.

### Cultural Lag
* **Definition:** A phenomenon where material culture (technology) advances faster than non-material culture (laws, morals, or norms).
* **Examples:**
  * **AI/Technology:** We build advanced tools but struggle to establish ethical rules and regulations.
  * **Social Media:** Instant global connectivity exists, but legal frameworks for privacy and misinformation are still catching up.
* **Consequences:** Can lead to social tension, conflict, or maladjustment until non-material aspects adapt.

## 🧠 Week 4: Socialization and Personality

### Socialization
* **Primary Socialization:** Early, intimate learning during childhood, primarily through the family, which teaches basic skills and core values.
* **Secondary Socialization:** Later, more formal learning through schools, peer groups, mass media, and religion.

### Personality Development
* **Nature vs. Nurture:** A fundamental debate in sociology. **Nature** refers to biological/genetic predispositions, while **Nurture** refers to environmental influences and socialization. Your materials emphasize *Nurture*—how agents of socialization shape how individuals think and act.
* **Influences:**
  * **Peer Groups:** Provide an arena for identity exploration and learning group norms.
  * **Mass Media:** Shapes perceptions of reality, beauty standards, and success.

## ⚖️ Week 5: Stratification and Inequality

### Social Stratification
* **Definition:** The patterned arrangement of roles and statuses in society, creating distinct layers based on three main dimensions (often associated with Max Weber):
  * **Class (Economic):** Wealth and income.
  * **Status (Social):** Prestige, honor, and respect.
  * **Power (Political):** Ability to enforce one's will over others.
* **Dimensions of Status:** Status can be *ascribed* (assigned at birth, e.g., race, royal lineage) or *achieved* (earned through effort and merit, e.g., education, career).

### Systems of Stratification
* **Closed Systems:** Low mobility where status is ascribed (e.g., Slavery, Caste, or Estate systems).
* **Open Systems:** Higher mobility based primarily on achieved status (e.g., Class systems in modern industrial societies).

### Social Inequality & Technology
* **Digital Divide:** Technology does not reach everyone equally; urban and educated populations often benefit more, while remote or poor communities struggle with access. This gap can worsen existing social inequalities.
